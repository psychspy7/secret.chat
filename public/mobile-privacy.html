Privacy, in plain language.

This page describes the SecretChat Android beta. The earlier browser chat is a separate service with a different storage model.

Your messages and room keys

The Android app encrypts message contents with AES-GCM before transmission. Private invitation codes unlock room keys, which remain in participants’ device storage. Chat history is encrypted in app-private storage using an Android Keystore key. Private rooms have no administrator decryption keys or cloud chat archive. Optional research rooms are visibly labeled and require the creator and each joining participant to agree to administrator access for training and research. In research rooms, the server retains up to 1,000 encrypted messages per room until an approved manual clear and a room invitation encrypted to the administrator’s research public key. The shared archive is capped at 4,096 messages across all research rooms, and pauses new research messages at capacity until an approved manual clear. Research messages have no automatic age-based deletion. The private research key is password-encrypted; the administrator unlocks it locally to view or export those rooms. Existing private rooms are not converted.

Messages arrive live. A message sent while your device is disconnected is not available to retrieve later. The app keeps up to 500 received messages per room on a device. Normal updates preserve local data. Uninstalling, clearing app data, losing your phone, or losing its encryption key removes that local history; Google or email sign-in alone does not restore it. Use the app’s local-history controls to remove saved messages. Any member may request shared clearing: every current non-disabled member must approve within 24 hours. A decline rejects it; a new member joining cancels it. The administrator may clear any room directly. Research archives are deleted, and updated apps clear local history when they reconnect. Existing copies, exports or older apps cannot be remotely erased. Older apps must update to resume sending after a shared clear.

Account and connection information

Google supplies account identity, including your email address and name, to Supabase Authentication. Email sign-in confirms access to your email address through a one-time sign-in link. SecretChat stores your chosen display name, user ID, room labels, membership and presence information, administration requests, and the last IP address observed by the backend. Kitty Corp.’s administrator can view and manage this account and room metadata. IP addresses describe a network connection and may be shared, proxied, or changed; they are not proof of a person’s identity.

Account and room metadata remains on the backend until removed through service administration. Creators choose a 1–24 hour room lifetime. The administrator may approve extensions or create groups without expiry; permanent groups still have capacity limits. Short-lived delivery records and notification queues are cleaned regularly; message identifier records expire after ten minutes and notification queue records after one day, with cleanup running hourly. Inactive push device records expire after sixty days.

Optional notifications

If you enable room alerts, Firebase Cloud Messaging receives a device delivery token and a generic room join, message, or app-update notification. Chat contents are never included in notification payloads. You can mute individual rooms or disable alerts in the app and Android Settings. Delivery and sounds depend on network access, Android notification permissions, battery settings, and device availability.

Service providers and your choices

Supabase operates authentication, room metadata, and the encrypted live relay. Google operates Google sign-in and Firebase notification delivery. These providers process connection and operational metadata to run their services. The app does not include advertising or analytics SDKs, and private-room contents are not collected for model training. Consented research-room messages may be viewed and exported by Kitty Corp. for training and research. Exported files are under the administrator’s control; manual clearing inside the service does not erase exported copies.

Keep invitation codes private: anyone who receives a valid code can enter its room and access future messages while connected. Participants can copy information or photograph a screen with another device. Screenshot protection is enabled for normal Android accounts. The verified administrator can capture their own unlocked foreground app screen; recent-task previews remain protected. Optional fingerprint or strong biometric unlock has device PIN/password recovery. Screenshot protection cannot make a compromised device secure.
